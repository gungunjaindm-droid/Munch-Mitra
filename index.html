The same simple process for every product.

How much boiling water?

Amounts are for one 60 g serving. Making more? Use the same amount of water for every 60 g. A 100 g pack makes about 1½ servings, a 250 g pack about 4, and a 500 g pack about 8.

ProductBoiling water per 60 g
Poha (Indori, Masala, Kanda)around 100 ml
Khichdi (Masala, Moong Dal), Sheeraaround 120 ml
Upma, Vermicelli, Oats, Dalia, Millet Bowl, Sabudana Khichdi, Ragi Maltaround 140 ml

Sabudana Khichdi needs around 140 ml, a little more than the other khichdis.

Breakfast ideas and tips from Munch Mitra.

5-minute breakfast ideas for busy morningsBreakfast ideas

Mornings are rushed, but breakfast does not have to be skipped. Keep a few packs in your drawer and pick one by mood.

Craving something tangy? Go for Masala Poha. Want something light and south Indian? Try Vegetable Upma. Need something filling before a long day? Oats or Moong Dal Khichdi will do. For a sweet start, a bowl of Sheera is ready in the same time.

Measure 60 g into a bowl, add boiling water, stir once, cover and wait 3 to 5 minutes. That is the whole recipe.

How to make perfect poha in a bowlTips

Use freshly boiled water so the poha softens evenly. Add around 100 ml for every 60 g, stir once, then cover the bowl so the steam stays in.

Wait the full 3 to 5 minutes before you open it. Fluff with a fork and add a squeeze of lemon and some fresh coriander if you like.

Poha, upma or khichdi: which one today?Guide

Poha is light, tangy and quick. Upma is soft, mildly spiced and comforting. Khichdi is warm and filling, great for lunch or dinner as well.

Not sure? Start with one of each in the 100 g pack and see which one becomes your regular.
